# EU-first infrastructure

If your users are in the EU, your data should be too. That sounds
ideological until you see what happens when it isn&apos;t: GDPR audits,
DPA requests you can&apos;t answer, CLOUD Act risk you can&apos;t mitigate,
EU buyers who ask "where is this hosted" and walk away when the answer is
us-east-1.

<MarkerHighlight>EU-first isn&apos;t marketing — it&apos;s an architectural decision made at setup time.</MarkerHighlight>{" "}
Bolting it on afterwards costs 10x more than starting that way.

## The minimum stack

For a SaaS product or mobile-app backend, the practical EU-first stack
that doesn&apos;t require self-hosting:

| Layer | Choice | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Database + auth | Supabase | EU-Frankfurt |
| Monitoring / error tracking | Sentry | EU-Frankfurt |
| Object storage | Cloudflare R2 | (S3-compat, dataresidency by config) |
| Email | Resend | EU instance |
| Payment | Stripe / Mollie | Stripe stores some PII US-side; Mollie is NL-native |
| Hosting (web) | Vercel | EU regions available, but check function regions |

Each has explicit EU-region options. Pick the EU option at setup; never
"upgrade later". Region migrations are expensive and risky.

## What the choice actually buys you

**Data residency.** Customer PII (emails, names, payment info, app
content) stays in the EU. Transfers to US-controlled entities only happen
under explicit DPAs or SCC fallbacks. This is what regulators look for.

**DPA clarity.** Every processor (the services above) provides a DPA you
can sign. Keep them in a `Verwerkers-Dienst-DPA` register —
spreadsheet is fine, just have it. When a customer asks "do you have a
DPA with Supabase", you say yes and produce it within a day.

**GDPR Art. 30 register.** A processing activities register
(`Verwerkingsregister`) listing every kind of personal data you process,
the purpose, the lawful basis, who you share it with, retention.
Boilerplate template, fill in your specifics, update when you change. It&apos;s
not optional; it just doesn&apos;t get audited until something goes
wrong.

**User-deletion path.** GDPR right-to-erasure isn&apos;t hard if your data
is in one place. Build the `/account/delete` endpoint on day one. Test
it. The first time a user asks, you don&apos;t want to be designing it
under pressure.

## CLOUD Act considerations

Even if your storage is in the EU, if the *operator* is a US company,
the CLOUD Act technically allows US law enforcement to compel data
production. This is a real risk for some industries (legal, health,
defense). For most consumer products, it&apos;s academic — but the
*positioning* matters to EU buyers.

The mitigations:

1. Pick EU-headquartered services where possible (Mollie over Stripe,
   Hetzner over AWS, OVHcloud over Azure)
2. Sign DPAs with SCC clauses that cover government-access scenarios
3. Document your sub-processor list publicly — transparency is a buyer
   trust signal

You don&apos;t need to be 100% pure-EU (Anthropic Claude API is US-only,
for example). You do need to be transparent about which services are
which.

## What this doesn&apos;t mean

EU-first doesn&apos;t mean *EU-only*. It means *EU-default with explicit
exceptions*. AI inference might run against a US Anthropic endpoint. CDN
might serve from anywhere. Some analytics tools may not have EU instances.
Document the exceptions, keep them minimal, ensure they don&apos;t handle
unencrypted PII.

## When to make this decision

At setup. Day one. Picking Supabase EU vs Supabase US at the first
`create project` click is a 5-second decision that saves a 5-month
migration later.

If you&apos;re already in a US region and want to switch: budget a
quarter, plan the cutover carefully, expect to lose some data
relationships or test environments during the switch. It&apos;s doable;
it&apos;s not fun.

## What EU-first signals to buyers

Two things, depending on the buyer:

1. **EU enterprise / public sector buyers:** they&apos;re *required* to
   buy EU-hosted by their compliance framework. Without EU-first, you&apos;re
   not in the conversation.

2. **Privacy-conscious consumers / creators:** they actively choose EU
   services after BandLab / Endlesss / cloud-collapse stories. Privacy
   becomes a feature, not just compliance.

Neither group is the majority of any market. Both will pay a premium for
the discipline. That&apos;s why EU-first works as a niche rather than just
as a constraint.

## What it costs you

Slightly more vendor friction (some tools EU-region is the secondary
choice). Slightly more careful processor selection. A `Verwerkings­register`
to maintain. About 4-8 hours of one-time setup work.

In return: a defensible position, an actual answer when EU buyers ask,
zero migration debt, and the ability to use "EU-first" as a positioning
claim without lying. Worth the setup.
